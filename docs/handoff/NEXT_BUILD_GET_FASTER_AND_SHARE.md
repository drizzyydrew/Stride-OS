## Athlete-facing changes

- **Get faster** is a selectable goal during onboarding and next-goal setup, without requiring a race. It survives the goal draft/archive paths and has its own summary label. Its macro plan organizes aerobic preparation, controlled sustained work, aerobic-power exposure and easier base practice in six-week blocks, with the existing separate recovery rhythm. Running sessions still require actual tolerated work before progressing; a later phase or an advanced experience label does not earn hard intervals. A base-only preference is respected.
- **Plyometrics** already existed in templates. New-version generated plans identify reactive calf hops and skipping as impact work as well as exercises labeled plyometric. Ungated template jumps are removed. Appropriate running/hybrid goals can receive one optional exposure after actual recent lower-body/calf practice, consistent running, suitable recovery, symptoms, time and phase checks. The default introduction is two sets of five small bilateral hops. Same-task progression requires three distinct practice days with actual manageable sets, explicitly controlled landings, no symptoms during the exercise and a usual response at least 24 hours afterward. Older larger doses cannot override the latest reduced dose.
- The catalog includes three easy, five medium and four hard tasks. These are relative task-demand guides. Higher tiers require an explicit **Consider harder** preference plus appropriate actual preparation; harder unilateral work also requires specific single-leg strength evidence and a sustained running foundation. Role-preserving paths include small → quicker → single-leg in-place pogo hops, and bilateral → unilateral forward or lateral hops. A new task starts with fewer contacts and its own response evidence. There is no generated external loading, depth jump or maximum-height jump escalation. Catalog availability does not mean every task is automatically selected or that exercises rotate randomly each week.
- Recovery weeks, poor recovery, an earned strength regression and unsuitable key-run spacing hold jumping. Adverse reports reset older higher-tier evidence; an unrelated tiny successful task cannot clear that reset. Current-week performed impact and active workouts prevent additional newly generated jumping. Saved actual sets and captured prescriptions remain intact. A regenerated completed-plan preview can omit its optional jump after the weekly hold; that preview is not a replacement for the saved workout record. Legacy programming remains behind its existing version boundary.
- Existing exercise-adjustment controls offer mechanically compatible, labeled choices, with lower/equal-demand alternatives for difficulty, dislike or equipment. Substitutions retain performed work, calibrate separately and cannot increase remaining contacts. The prescription explains full recovery, reducing work or skipping it. Ordinary calf strength can support preparation but is explicitly a different stimulus, not an equivalent plyometric replacement.
- Saved strength Activity details have a collapsed **Jump response** for an exact, uniquely identified completed reactive exercise. Control, symptoms and next-day answers default to unknown; Repeat / Make easier / Consider harder is a separate preference. The next-day choices become available after 24 hours and refresh on reopening. Local readback precedes publication and existing sync, owner/athlete changes retire stale drafts and callbacks, and only that exercise's optional response is patched. Missing historical responses are not backfilled.
- **Activity detail → Share image → Story → Share Details → Story illustration** offers None, Route and Everyday equivalent when their data is available. It is one choice, so the route and equivalent cannot appear together. The equivalent occupies the existing route area; the amount and appliance text use one size and color. Source/estimate and power-versus-energy notes stay in the exported canvas. Select All preserves the chosen illustration or prefers the non-location comparison; Clear removes it. Square format does not render either Story illustration.
- Everyday comparisons reuse validated saved work/power quantities and existing reference math. They do not convert calories to fabricated output, recalculate a new profile estimate, write activity metrics or invent measurements. The reference is stable per activity for consistent preview/export. Missing/invalid quantities leave the option unavailable. No existing image assets are edited or created.

## Evidence and boundaries

The [NSCA plyometric exercise guidance](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/plyometric-exercises/) emphasizes a sport-relevant stretch-shortening stimulus, movement mastery, low-volume quality work and the athlete's other training demands. The [2026 NSCA position statement, pages 7–8](https://www.nsca.com/globalassets/about/position-statements/national-strength-and-conditioning-association-position-statement-on-strength-and-conditioning-of-female-athletes.-part-ii.pdf) describes intensity as involving several mechanical and athlete factors; contact counts alone are insufficient. Its athlete population and supervised-training context do not validate an app-based medical clearance system.

The [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/) supports specificity, progressive stimulus and individualization around experience, goals, needs and preferences. It does not prescribe the app's exact jump tiers, next-day questionnaire or numeric thresholds. Those are explicit coaching/product choices, not an ACSM-approved protocol.

The [randomized concurrent-training study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37062901/) compared running plus plyometric work with running plus dynamic bodyweight work in 31 male recreational runners over eight weeks. It does not establish one universally superior approach, automatic eligibility for every athlete or guaranteed faster running. Reported symptoms/control and next-day response are athlete reports, not measurements of tissue capacity. The app's particular contact counts, report requirements, 24-hour response-recording boundary and recovery cutoffs are not clinical clearance criteria.

The exercise inventory contains 152 canonical entries, including text-only entries for the new tasks. Existing artwork is retained. The default generated path and compatible alternatives preserve the practiced role; power skips and repeated vertical work can also be recognized through their own known task history rather than being injected as random variety.
