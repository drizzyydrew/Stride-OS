# Activity-specific training content

The new profiles use the existing adaptive plan, not independent plans added together. Movement selection is a coaching choice informed by the task, the person's available equipment and capacity, and actual performance/recovery. Strength, dynamic balance, mobility and impact contacts remain different doses. Names do not establish transfer to sport or injury prevention.

## Foot modules

- **Foot Control & Balance:** seated toe pressure (2 × 6 holds of 5 seconds), supported clock reaches (2 rounds of one reach in each of three directions per side; six reaches per side total), supported straight-knee calf raises (2 × 8–12, manageable effort).
- **Foot & Calf Strength:** the small clock dose while fresh, loaded supported single-leg calf raises (3 × 6–8 per side, around RPE 8 when appropriate), loaded seated/bent-knee raises (2 × 8–12, around RPE 7). Introductory or recovery doses reduce sets/effort and use a supported/bilateral alternative when useful. Loaded seated raises require meaningful resistance relative to the person; unloaded repetitions are a warm-up/lower-capacity option. No fixed pound threshold or muscle-isolation claim.
- **Reactive Feet & Ankles:** first exposure is only 2 × 5 small two-foot pogo landings, with full rests. The practiced template is 3 × 5 pogos plus 2 × 5 small forward/back landings (25 landing events, 50 individual foot contacts), not out-and-back pairs. Two consecutive complete, controlled impact exposures with an honest later/next-day response inform one incremental step (10 to 15 to 20 to 25 landing events). A newer negative, uncertain or missing response returns the recommendation to the introduction. A 42-day record window is a conservative app design choice, not a validated clearance test. This replaces an existing impact dose; it is not extra conditioning. Lifting experience alone does not establish plyometric tolerance.

New running/outdoor strength sessions also retain a small clock-reach primer and direct calf role within the existing time budget. In a short 30-minute session, one round of three directions per side can replace lower-priority accessories; the two-round preset remains available. The primer is counted once and does not add an extra training day.

Clock reaches use firm support as needed, stable stance-foot contact, controlled hip/knee/trunk movement and the farthest *controlled* reach. No push-off from the tapping foot. Progress reach/support/directions separately. Balance practice does not replace loaded unilateral strength, and support is permitted when strength is the priority.

Short-foot practice is optional: imagine the foot as a small tent, with heel and bases of the big/little toes as its corners; keep corners grounded and gently lift the middle with long toes. A simpler toe press supplies a related but different pressure task. Neither is a prerequisite for running.

## Outdoor emphasis

Hiking emphasizes ascent, controlled lowering, carrying and gradual actual terrain exposure. Downhill skiing includes unilateral leg force, lateral loading and selected local isometric endurance. Snowboarding combines leg force, lateral loading and trunk control without forcing board stance on the gym floor. Cross-country skiing includes leg and upper-body poling support plus clearly labelled whole-body aerobic practice. Mountaineering emphasizes sustained uphill work, carrying, unilateral leg force and descent tolerance. Actual sport exposure consumes the same training budget. Beginners get suitable supported variants; no elite training volume is copied.

Preparation uses comfortable ankle/hip/trunk/shoulder movement selected for the task, followed by easy task-specific movement and rehearsal sets. Cooldowns are optional comfort choices, not promises to prevent soreness or injury.

## Primary sources and limits

- [ACSM 2026 resistance-training position stand](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897): goal-specific, individualized dose. Repetition ranges overlap; neither a weekly average of 80–85% nor attendance alone establishes effective strength training or progression.
- [NSCA plyometric guidance](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/plyometric-exercises/): purpose, quality, low volume, adequate recovery and other sport contacts matter. The app's exact module dose is a coaching choice.
- [Standing versus seated calf trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38156065/): standing produced greater gastrocnemius growth in this small untrained sample; soleus changes were similar. This does not show that heavy seated calf work is useless or establish universal sport superiority.
- [Foot training proof-of-concept trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31962191/) and [runner foot-core trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33156692/): support considering direct foot work; they do not validate this exact app prescription for every user.
- [Secondary running-biomechanics trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35497357/): do not promise reduced impact from foot training.
- [Balance task specificity](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39992961/): reach practice is not a guarantee of running or snow-sport performance transfer.
- [Nordic ski physiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34993468/), [ski-mountaineering testing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34208925/) and [recreational alpine ski observations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9408581/): inform task demands, not validation of a complete automated program, elite volume targets or an injury-risk score.
