# StrideOS Training Engine Periodization Review

Date: 2026-07-19  
Scope: beginner preset goals, run/walk progression, strength and cross-training placement, and future high-intensity interval eligibility.

## Sources reconciled

This review translates accepted endurance-programming principles into StrideOS engine rules without copying book text. It reconciles:

- NSCA / CSCS endurance and periodization guidance: specificity, progressive overload, recovery, strength support, and fatigue management.
- Matt Fitzgerald / 80-20 intensity distribution principles: most endurance volume should remain easy, with hard work introduced sparingly and intentionally.
- Daniels-style training organization: easy running, threshold, interval, repetition concepts, and the need to match workouts to current ability.
- Steve Magness-style coaching principles: avoid formulaic load rules, respond to athlete context, and build durable capacity before chasing intensity.
- Strength Running / Jason Fitzgerald principles: consistency, injury-resilient strength, gradual long-run development, and conservative beginner progression.
- Run Elite / Andrew Snow principles as implemented in StrideOS: clear session purpose, hard/easy separation, and purposeful rather than random intensity.
- Norwegian 4×4 interval research: four 4-minute high-intensity intervals can be a useful aerobic-power stimulus for prepared athletes, but it is not a beginner foundation default.
- Existing StrideOS philosophy: Observation → Interpretation → Recommendation, conservative evidence language, and adaptive training based on readiness, symptoms, adherence, and load.

## 1. Beginner foundation rules

- Maximum one primary running exposure per day.
- Beginner foundation plans default to easy effort, walking, or run/walk.
- No threshold, VO2, race-pace, Norwegian 4×4, or hard consecutive running days in early foundation phases.
- Progress frequency and duration independently; do not use a rigid 10% rule as an automatic prescription.
- A current-day session cannot be marked missed until the local day has ended.

## 2. Run/walk progression rules

- Every run/walk session must include warm-up, run interval, walk interval, rounds, cooldown, HR target, RPE, pace guidance, and voice-prompt timing.
- Progress by increasing total duration, run-interval duration, or reducing walk recovery, but not all aggressively at once.
- Run/walk completion can remain valid without requiring continuous running.
- Hold or repeat a week when symptoms, readiness, or adherence indicate the athlete is not absorbing the current step.

## 3. Easy-volume distribution

- Early plans prioritize easy aerobic work and movement consistency.
- Hard work is withheld until the athlete has enough recent consistency and recovery capacity.
- Cycling, swimming, and other cross-training contribute whole-body load but are not converted into running mileage.

## 4. Long-run progression

- Long sessions progress gradually and remain conversational.
- For beginner plans, the long session may be walk, run/walk, or easy run depending on current mode.
- Long-session progression should be reduced in recovery weeks or after missed key sessions.

## 5. Recovery-week logic

- Recovery weeks appear regularly when accumulated load warrants them.
- Recovery weeks reduce duration and avoid adding intensity.
- Missed-session adaptation avoids cramming skipped work into the remaining week.

## 6. Strength placement

- Strength is supporting unless explicitly selected as the primary session.
- Beginner strength should support running economy, tissue capacity, and movement quality without compromising the next key endurance day.
- Prefer separating strength and beginner running when soreness, adherence, or recovery risk is high.

## 7. Cross-training placement

- Easy cycling, swimming, elliptical, hiking, and walking can supplement or replace easy aerobic work depending on athlete preference and readiness.
- HIIT and mixed-modal work count as intensity and should not be stacked next to hard running or heavy strength.
- Seasonal sports can contribute load when available, but do not replace impact-specific progression unless the plan explicitly says so.

## 8. Threshold introduction criteria

Threshold work requires:

- Athlete is beyond beginner foundation.
- Recent consistency is adequate.
- Easy volume is tolerated.
- No unresolved symptom or readiness concern.
- No conflicting high-intensity session nearby.

## 9. VO2 introduction criteria

VO2-oriented work requires:

- More training maturity than threshold introduction.
- Consistent easy and lower-intensity interval tolerance.
- Adequate recovery and readiness.
- No hard-day stacking.

## 10. Norwegian 4×4 eligibility criteria

Norwegian 4×4 is an advanced template only:

- Progressive warm-up.
- Four 4-minute work intervals.
- Three-minute active recovery between intervals.
- Work intensity approximately 85–95% HRmax when appropriate.
- Cooldown.

Automatic scheduling remains disabled unless:

- The athlete is not in a beginner foundation plan.
- Progression level is intermediate or advanced.
- Recent consistency and aerobic base are adequate.
- Lower-intensity intervals have been tolerated.
- No high-intensity conflict is nearby.
- Readiness and symptom state support intensity.
- An explicit feature flag enables scheduling.

## 11. Same-day training rules

- Beginner plans: one primary running session per day.
- Same-day strength is allowed only as supporting work when total stress remains appropriate.
- Same-day cross-training must be clearly labeled as replacement or supplement.
- Optional mobility can coexist with a primary session when low load.

## 12. Hard-day spacing

- No hard running on consecutive days for beginners.
- Advanced workouts require spacing from other high-load sessions.
- If readiness is low, downgrade or move intensity rather than preserving the template blindly.

## 13. Missed-session adaptation

- Do not compress missed workouts into the remainder of a week.
- Preserve completed history.
- Recalculate nearby sessions to protect recovery and hard/easy separation.

## 14. Readiness adaptation

- Low readiness reduces duration, intensity, or both.
- Symptoms bias toward walking, recovery, or clinician-directed modification.
- High readiness may permit planned work, but does not override progression gates.

## 15. Race-distance-specific progression

- 5K: time-based run/walk first, then gradually longer continuous running if tolerated.
- 10K: extend total duration after consistency is established.
- Half marathon: build base and long-session durability before meaningful intensity.
- Marathon: long-term progression with walking/running tolerance, fueling practice, strength preservation, and cross-training as support.

## 16. Evidence limitations

StrideOS training rules are coaching logic, not a medical prescription. They cannot guarantee race completion, prevent injury, diagnose symptoms, or determine tissue loading. Recommendations should remain adjustable based on athlete response, local conditions, and appropriate clinical guidance when symptoms or medical concerns are present.
