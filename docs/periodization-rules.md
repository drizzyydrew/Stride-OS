# StrideOS Periodization Rules

Build 45 establishes this document as the coaching constitution for training engines, goal-based training paths, custom plans, AI-generated plans, adaptive plans, workout alternatives, AI Coach context, and future native/watch surfaces.

## Core principle

StrideOS progresses training only when the athlete has earned the next signal. Calendar time alone is not evidence of readiness. Completion history, planned-versus-completed data, readiness, recent load, session stress, available days, interruption context, and phase intent must agree before progression.

## Weekly decision vocabulary

- `progress`: increase one training lever because recent execution, readiness, and recovery support it.
- `maintain`: keep the current prescription stable when training is working but the evidence does not justify more.
- `regress`: reduce load or complexity when recent execution suggests the current prescription is too much.
- `deload`: intentionally reduce total stress to absorb previous training.
- `repeat`: repeat the current week when consistency or recovery is the best next step.
- `rebuild`: reconstruct the remainder of the week after interruptions, missed clusters, or conflicting schedule changes.

## Hard, medium, easy, and recovery philosophy

Every session should have a computed stress profile with separate axes for cardio, lower muscular, upper muscular, impact, neuromuscular demand, and duration. A hard upper-body strength day is not the same stress as hill repetitions. A long easy run is not the same stress as VO₂ intervals. Calendar protections and adaptations must compare axes rather than broad labels only.

Easy sessions stay easy. Accessory volume, hidden pace chasing, or replacement activities must not upgrade an easy day into a quality day without an explicit plan change and preview.

## Deload philosophy

Default rhythm is three loading weeks plus one deload week. Deload volume defaults near 70% of normal and should stay in the 65–75% range unless a future engine has a documented reason to choose otherwise. Taper weeks are not overwritten by automatic deload logic.

## Progress, maintain, regress

Progression should change the smallest useful lever. Avoid increasing volume, intensity, frequency, and strength load at the same time. When the engine does progress more than one lever, it must emit a rationale.

Maintain is the default safe decision when evidence is mixed.

Regression is appropriate after repeated partial completions, stopped-early sessions, unusually high RPE, poor recovery, symptom flags, or load spikes.

## Week 1 restrictions

Week 1 of a plan or rebuilt block must not introduce formal quality. It may use easy running, run/walk, walking, mobility, strides for experienced athletes, and low-load strength only.

## Quality progression

The default quality ladder is:

1. Easy running
2. Strides
3. Gentle fartlek
4. Hills
5. Tempo or threshold
6. Intervals
7. Norwegian 4×4

Newer athletes should move one rung at a time. Experienced athletes may re-enter higher on the ladder when documented history supports it.

## Norwegian 4×4 eligibility

Norwegian 4×4 is never assigned in beginner foundation phases and never assigned by elapsed time alone. Eligibility requires an established aerobic base, proven sustained-intensity tolerance, controlled HR/RPE response, stable recovery, a suitable phase and goal, and no adjacent hard lower-body stress.

## Strength periodization

Strength supports the running goal. Foundation emphasizes movement quality and durability. Base emphasizes force production. Build converts strength toward power and running economy. Race-specific, peak, and taper phases reduce volume and avoid high fatigue. Deload and recovery phases use prehab, activation, or mobility emphasis.

High-volume hypertrophy must not be placed before key long runs or high-impact quality days.

## Taper rules

Taper protects freshness. Do not add hard load because an athlete feels anxious or wants to “test fitness.” Taper may preserve short neuromuscular sharpness but should reduce total stress.

## Recovery rules

Recovery is a training output, not a failure state. Recovery weeks and repeated weeks should be explained plainly. The app may say: “This week intentionally repeats last week’s volume. Progress does not require increasing every week.”

## Workout substitutions

Substitutions should preserve the session intent when possible:

- Hills can become treadmill incline repetitions.
- Easy running can become easy cycling or walking when lower impact is needed.
- Strength can become dumbbell or bodyweight variants when equipment changes.
- Recovery can become walking, mobility, or active recovery.

The system must record the original session, chosen alternative, reason, whether intent was preserved, and completion classification. It must not fabricate measured distance, pace, HR, or power.

## Continuous 5K eligibility

Run-continuously half-marathon and marathon plans require evidence that the athlete can already run at least 5K continuously. Athletes below that threshold should be guided toward Couch to 5K, run/walk half or marathon plans, or a foundation block.

## Quality rotation

Quality exposure should be targeted, not maximal. One appropriate exposure every 7–10 days is usually enough once eligible. The goal is not to cycle through every quality type as quickly as possible.

## Calendar protections

The validator must warn on consecutive hard sessions, long runs immediately after hard lower-body strength, two primary runs on one day, insufficient recovery spacing, duplicate session IDs, movement outside plan boundaries, and locked-day conflicts. Cross-week moves must preserve stable scheduled-session identity.
